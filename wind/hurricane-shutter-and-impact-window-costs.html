What do official sources actually say about hurricane shutter and impact window costs in Florida?

Official sources tell you which opening protections count on the state insurance-discount form, what My Safe Florida Home can pay toward recommended opening protection, and that local permits and licensed contractors are required. They do not publish typical Florida prices for shutters or impact windows. Costs vary by home. Get written quotes from licensed contractors. What counts on the OIR-B1-1802 form

Florida insurers use the Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form, OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26), to record storm-protection features, including opening protection. We opened the official form on November 23, 2026. A fuller plain-language map of the form is on our wind-inspection form page. You can also open the official OIR-B1-1802 form (PDF).

Question 9 on the form is “Opening Protection.” The form records the weakest wind-borne debris protection on the structure. It covers windows, doors, sliding glass doors, skylights, and garage doors. It does not cover gable or other roof vents. The form grades verified impact-resistant products, certain plywood or OSB panels that meet a 2007 Florida Building Code table, unverified coverings, no protection, or damaged openings that need repair or replacement. Buildings with openings that need replacement, the form says, “do not qualify for wind mitigation rate differentials,” even if other features are present.

The form itself is not a price sheet. It also says some items listed on it may not qualify for a discount. Your insurer decides what discount, if any, applies to your policy. See how the insurance discounts work.

What My Safe Florida Home will pay toward them

My Safe Florida Home is a separate state grant program, described on our My Safe Florida Home grants page. A grant is not an insurance discount, and a free program inspection is not a grant approval.

Section 215.5586, Florida Statutes (2026), opened November 23, 2026, allows grant funds — when recommended by the initial inspection — for opening-protection improvements, including exterior doors, garage doors, windows, and skylights. The official 2025-26 Homeowner’s Guide also lists glass blocks among the openings discussed in the inspection report, and it says an applicant may be recommended to install impact-rated windows or shutters, or impact-rated exterior doors or garage doors.

Official program documents are explicit about one limit: the grant cannot be used to exchange one type of opening protection for another. The official article “What Improvements are authorized for Mitigation Projects?” and the 2025-26 Homeowner’s Guide both say that if you already have compliant hurricane shutters, a request for disbursement for impact windows on those openings will be denied, because the home already has protection against hurricane damage. An official “Improvement 1.0 — Opening Protection” article, opened November 23, 2026, says you do not have to replace every opening to receive grant funding, as long as the openings you upgrade are listed in the initial inspection report. Only recommended openings are eligible.

On payment amounts, the 2026 statute says grants must be matched $1 from the applicant for $2 from the state, up to a maximum state contribution of $10,000 toward the actual cost of the mitigation project, except that low-income applicants, as defined in section 420.0004(11), may receive up to $10,000 with no matching amount. The 2025-26 Homeowner’s Guide says the matching grant is a reimbursement of two-thirds of total project costs, up to that $10,000 state contribution, subject to legislative appropriation, and that a grant application must be approved before any work begins.

The official guide and a program support article include example calculations in which an eligible applicant “paid $15,000,” “paid $9,000,” or “paid $20,000” for recommended impact-rated windows, then received $10,000, $6,000, or $10,000 in grant funds. Those numbers are examples of how the match is calculated. They are not official typical prices, and we are not presenting them as what windows usually cost in Florida.

The 2026 statute also says the initial inspection report must include “a range of cost estimates regarding the recommended mitigation improvements.” That range is for the inspected house. It is not a published statewide price table, and we have not seen an official public list of typical shutter or window costs.

Permits and building-code basics

Florida law requires permits and licensed contractors for this work. Section 215.5586(2)(d), Florida Statutes (2026), says all hurricane mitigation performed under the My Safe Florida Home program “must be based upon the securing of all required local permits and inspections and must be performed by properly licensed contractors.” The grant application must include the intended contractor’s name and state license number, which the program must verify.

The official 2025-26 Homeowner’s Guide says homeowners must ensure contractors receive all applicable building permits from the local building inspector’s office. An official program article, “Who is responsible for obtaining building permits?”, modified July 9 and opened by us on November 23, 2026, says the same, and adds that you must confirm all permits are closed out and that the local building inspector’s office has completed all required building inspections before you request a final inspection from the program’s inspection company. The guide notes that a local building inspection is separate from the program’s final inspection.

The official OIR-B1-1802 form itself points to the Florida Building Code when it grades certain plywood or OSB opening coverings against a 2007 Florida Building Code table. We are not reprinting that table here. Use the form, and ask your local building department which code rules apply to your house and your project.

We did not find a single official statewide fee schedule for shutter or window permits. Permit rules and fees are set locally. Ask your county or city building department, and get the permit requirements in writing from the licensed contractor who would do the work.

What we could not source on prices

As of November 23, 2026, the official form, the 2026 statute, and the official My Safe Florida Home documents we opened do not publish typical, average, or “going rate” prices for hurricane shutters or impact windows in Florida. We are not going to invent those numbers, borrow them from sales sites, or treat the grant-match examples as market data.

Costs vary by the house, the openings, the product, the labor, and local permit requirements. If you are considering this work, get written quotes from licensed contractors, confirm whether the product meets the standard your insurer and the form will recognize, and confirm any grant or insurance-discount question with the official program or your insurer.

Before you pay for work

  • Get written quotes from properly licensed contractors. Do not rely on this page for a price.
  • Ask what the form and your insurer will recognize. The official Improvement 1.0 article tells applicants to ask their insurance company whether they need an A1, A2, or A3 rating for a discount, and to look at the initial inspection report for the openings that need improvement.
  • If you hope to use a My Safe Florida Home grant, official documents say the work must be recommended first, the grant must be approved before work begins, and compliant shutters already in place cannot be swapped for impact windows with grant funds. See current portal status on the grants page.
  • Get the required local permits and keep them closed out before a program final inspection.
