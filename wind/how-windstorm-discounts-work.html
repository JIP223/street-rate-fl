Insurance discounts · Fla. Stat. § 627.711

Do Florida storm-protection discounts cut the whole insurance bill?

No. The Florida Department of Financial Services says these insurance discounts apply only to the windstorm coverage of your policy, not the rest of the bill. Insurers must tell you about the discounts and must accept a valid state inspection form. Homes built to the 2001 Florida Building Code — or the 1994 South Florida Building Code in Miami-Dade and Broward — are, in the DFS July 2026 guide, automatically eligible for a minimum 68% discount on that windstorm portion. That is not 68% off the whole policy. Ask your insurer what it will apply to your policy. This page is not insurance advice.

What “insurance discount” means on this page

Florida law often says “premium discount, credit, other rate differential, or reduction in deductibles.” Those are related tools an insurer can use. On this page we say insurance discount for the reduction applied to premium, unless we are quoting the statute. A “credit” in the statute is not a separate public program and is not a discount off the entire bill.

The discount is only on the windstorm part of the premium

The DFS guide states this in plain words: “These discounts apply only to the windstorm coverage of your policy.”

That matches how Florida law treats the bill itself. Section 627.0629(4), Florida Statutes (2026), says a rate filing for residential property insurance “shall be separated into two components, rates for hurricane coverage and rates for all other coverages,” and that the premium notice “shall separately indicate the premium for hurricane coverage and the premium for all other coverages.”

So a large-looking percentage can still leave most of a bill untouched if a large share of the premium is not windstorm coverage. We aren’t putting up a sample dollar split, because we don’t have your policy and we don’t invent figures.

What section 627.711 actually requires

Section 627.711 is the notice-and-form statute. It does not, by itself, set the 68% figure. It requires, among other things, that:

  • The insurer clearly notify you, when a personal lines residential policy is issued and at each renewal, of the availability and the range of each premium discount, credit, other rate differential, or reduction in deductibles — and combinations of those — for properties on which fixtures or construction techniques shown to reduce windstorm loss can be or have been installed. The prescribed form “shall describe generally what actions the policyholders may be able to take to reduce their windstorm premium.”
  • A uniform inspection form, developed by rule, “shall be used by all insurers when submitted by policyholders for the purpose of factoring discounts for wind insurance.” An insurer shall accept as valid a form signed by an authorized inspector listed in the statute. Who may sign, and how long the form can remain valid, is explained on our form page.
  • At its expense, the insurer may require that form to be independently verified before accepting it as valid.

DFS says admitted insurers must provide the Notice of Premium Discounts for Hurricane Loss Mitigation with any new residential property policy and at each renewal, and that since July 1, 2008, admitted carriers must accept a valid uniform form completed and signed by certain professional licensees.

The listed features insurers must include in their rate filings

The duty to offer the discounts, and the list of features that must be considered, sits in a companion statute, section 627.0629, not in section 627.711. Section 627.0629(1) says it is the Legislature’s intent “that insurers provide savings to consumers who install or implement windstorm damage mitigation techniques.” A rate filing for residential property insurance “must include actuarially reasonable discounts, credits, or other rate differentials, or appropriate reductions in deductibles,” for properties on which fixtures or construction techniques shown to reduce windstorm loss have been installed or implemented.

Those fixtures or techniques “must include, but are not limited to”:

  • wind uplift prevention
  • roof strength
  • roof covering performance
  • roof-to-wall strength
  • wall-to-floor-to-foundation strength
  • opening protection
  • window, door, and skylight strength

The same subsection says credits, discounts, or other rate differentials, or appropriate deductible reductions, for fixtures and techniques that meet the minimum requirements of the Florida Building Code must be included in the rate filing. Effective October 1, 2023, each insurer subject to that section must also provide information on its website describing the hurricane-mitigation discounts available to policyholders.

The amount is not one statewide table for every feature. DFS tells you this directly: “The amount of the discount will vary based on the wind mitigation features incorporated into your home. Your insurance agent and/or insurance company should be able to inform you how much of a discount you may expect if certain wind mitigation features are installed and/or upgraded.” The Office of Insurance Regulation’s wind-mitigation resources page, opened the same day, still lists older “range of wind premium credits” forms (OIR-B1-1699 and OIR-B1-1700, effective October 2006) as “updates pending.” We are not copying feature-by-feature percentages from those older forms while OIR flags them as pending updates.

The 68% figure — exact wording, and what it is not

The July 2026 DFS guide says:

“Homes built in compliance with the 2001 Florida Building Code or later edition, including the 1994 South Florida Building Code for homes in Miami-Dade and Broward Counties, are automatically eligible for a minimum 68% discount on the windstorm coverage of the premium due to the upgraded requirements which include a roof covering, roof attachment and roof to wall connection. You may be eligible for a greater discount if other mitigation features are installed on your home.”

Read that sentence carefully:

  • It is a minimum discount, not a cap, and not a promise of a larger number.
  • It applies to the windstorm coverage of the premium, not to the whole policy and not to non-wind coverages.
  • It is described as automatic for homes built in compliance with those codes, because of upgraded roof covering, roof attachment, and roof-to-wall connection requirements.
  • Other features — the guide discusses hip roofs and opening protection in this later-code section — may mean a greater discount, but the guide does not publish a second statewide percentage for those extras. Ask your insurer.

Never treat this as “68% off the policy.” If someone advertises it that way, they are not following the DFS wording we opened.

The same guide also says that, regardless of the year of construction, if a home meets the minimum wind-mitigation requirements of the 2001 Florida Building Code, you may be eligible for the premium discounts and “may also have the option to reduce your hurricane deductible.” Whether that option is open on your policy is a question for your insurer, not for this page.

Features the DFS guide says may affect the discount

For homes built before the 2001 codes, the DFS guide says discounts are based on roof covering, roof-deck attachment, roof-to-wall connections (it describes clips, single wraps/straps, and double straps), a hip roof shape, secondary water resistance, and opening protection. It describes two shutter levels in that guide: an “Intermediate Type” that meets half the pre-2001 Florida Building Code standards for South Florida, and a “Hurricane Protection Type” that meets the post-2001 Florida Building Code standards for South Florida.

For homes built in compliance with the 2001 code or later, the same guide says the additional discounts described there are based on roof shape and those shutter types, on top of the minimum 68% on the windstorm coverage already discussed.

The current inspection form uses its own labels and tests for opening protection (verified impact products, certain plywood or OSB panels, unverified coverings, none, or damaged openings). Those form categories are on the form page. We are not collapsing the form and the consumer guide into one unofficial chart.

Before you rely on a discount you saw online

  • Look at your premium notice for the separate hurricane or windstorm charge, not only the total.
  • Get a current, signed state form if you do not already have a valid one. Inspections on or after April 1, 2026 must use OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26).
  • Send the form to your insurer and ask, in writing, what insurance discount it will apply to the windstorm portion of your premium.
  • Do not add percentages together from a blog or a sales flyer. DFS says the amount varies, and your insurer files its own discounts.
  • Confirm any 68% claim is being applied only to windstorm coverage, and only if your home actually meets the code condition in the DFS guide.
  • Ask your insurer, not this site, what your premium and deductible will be after the form is reviewed.
